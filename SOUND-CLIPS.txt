FORMAT
- MP3, 128 kbps, stereo or mono.
- Loops: 60-90 seconds. Trim so the end flows into the start; the code adds a
  2-second crossfade, so small seams are fine.
- Effects: 0.3-3 seconds. Trim silence off the front so they play the instant you click.
- Keep total audio under about 10 MB.

COPYRIGHT - READ THIS FIRST
The site will be a public link. Clips of real songs (80s hits, radio broadcasts,
movie or TV audio) are copyrighted and can get the site taken down or disqualified.
For the three radio stations use ONLY:
  - music you or a friend play and record yourselves, or
  - tracks marked free to use / CC0 (for example Pixabay Music or the YouTube
    Audio Library), and keep a note of each track's name and source for the credits.
Effects you record yourselves (a real mug, a real kettle) are the safest and most fun.
If you take effects from freesound.org, use ones marked CC0 and note the link.

LOOPS
------------------------------------------------------------
FILE                      CHANNEL    WHAT TO CAPTURE
loop-fire.mp3             Ambiance   Wood fire crackling in a stove or fireplace. Steady, no pops louder than the rest.
loop-clock.mp3            Ambiance   Slow wall clock ticking, one tick per second, quiet room.
loop-rain.mp3             Ambiance   Soft steady rain on a window. No thunder.
loop-snow.mp3             Ambiance   Faint winter wind outside a closed window.
loop-purr.mp3             Ambiance   A cat purring steadily in its sleep, close and soft. No meows. (Added in M6.)
radio-softrock.mp3        Music      Mellow instrumental soft rock in an early-80s style. No vocals.
radio-country.mp3         Music      Gentle instrumental country, acoustic guitar. No vocals.
radio-jazz.mp3            Music      Soft late-night jazz, piano or trio. No vocals.

Each Ambiance loop has its own on/off switch in the almanac (rain and snow share
the one called Weather), as well as the shared Ambiance volume slider.

Tip for the radio: run the three music loops through an "AM radio" or
"telephone" EQ preset (cut below 300 Hz and above 4 kHz) so they sound like
they come out of Grandma's radio rather than headphones.
